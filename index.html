Nova and Aala 01 - The Thinning

Now, if you ever find yourself standing at the edge of a moonwell in Shadowglen, take a moment before you do anything else. Just look at it. It's so silver and so still that it seems to be quietly correcting the moon's homework, and it has been doing this, patiently, since long before anyone thought to build a path to it.

It was beside this moonwell that Nova stood one morning, reading a note for the third time, as if reading it a fourth time might make it more helpful.

"Conservator Ilthalaine requests your assistance at the moonwell clearing. Bring your staff."

That was all it said. No explanation. No "dear Nova, hope this finds you well." Just an order, politely disguised as a request, the way orders from conservators so often are.

Nova was, by nature, the sort of person who liked to know things before she did them. Not knowing things made her nervous, and being nervous made her hold her spellbook a little too tightly against her chest, which is exactly what she was doing when she crossed the root-bridge and saw that someone had gotten there before her.

The someone was a draenei, robed plainly, staff planted in the moss with the easy confidence of a person who has never once worried about looking silly. They were watching the moonwell with the patient expression of someone who had already decided the day was going to be interesting, one way or another.

"You got a note too," said the draenei, without turning around. It wasn't really a question.

"I did," said Nova. "I don't suppose you know what it's about."

"Not the faintest idea." A pause, and then, over one shoulder: "I'm Aala. I was told to bring my staff, so either we're being tested, or somebody very important probably misplaced something very small."

Nova laughed without giving it the first thought, which she found almost as surprising as the joke itself. "Nova," she named herself, and didn't get the chance to say much else, because that was precisely the moment Conservator Ilthalaine arrived, moss-robed, staff in hand, moving like someone who had already had a very long morning, still had a long staff load of tasks to get through, and that was just fine with her.

Now, a word about Ilthalaine. She was the sort of elder who had clearly seen every kind of trouble a forest could produce, filed it neatly away, and was fully prepared to see it all again. She did not waste words. She did not waste time. And when she pointed her staff out past the water, both apprentices understood, instantly, that whatever was wrong was wrong in a big way, and was to be their job to fix.

"The spring rains were generous this year," Ilthalaine said. "Too generous." Beyond the moonwell, the ferns were doing something ferns were not supposed to do, trembling with far too much traffic. A trio of thistle boars shouldered past each other like impatient customers at a very small bakery. Somewhere in the shadows, a nightsaber slid past and vanished, the way nightsabers do, as if the forest had simply blinked.

The problem was though, trembling leads to trampling and the plants of the glen were stressed. This kind of the thing gets the attention of Conservators.

"Boar and nightsaber alike have multiplied beyond what the glen can feed," Ilthalaine went on. "If they are not encouraged gently outward, they will starve, or turn on one another for what little is left."

Nova's mind, which liked things sorted into tidy piles, sorted this immediately. "So we're not hunting them. We're moving them along."

"Just so. Thin the crowded clusters. Send them to further out. Just enough of them to restore the balance." Ilthalaine looked at her, really looked, the way conservators do when they want you to remember something. "No more."

Aala's grip on their staff tightened, just slightly. "And if someone decides that enough is not enough?" they asked. "If someone decides restraint is inconvenient?"

"Then I am trusting you," said Ilthalaine, "to be the one who remembers it isn't."

That seemed to settle something in Aala, though not entirely, because Aala turned to Nova next, studying her the way you might study a stranger you've just been handed a rope and told to trust with your life. Then, apparently satisfied, Aala grinned.

"So. Do we restore balance," they said gravely. "...Or convert excess boar to bacon sandwiches?"

Nova blinked. "I'm sorry, what?"

"Nothing. Just checking you were listening." And there it was, Nova's first real look at the fact that she had just been paired, for reasons unknown, with someone who used jokes the way other people used shields.

They set off together down the root-lined path, wisps drifting between the roots like lazy lanterns, and Nova spent several strides deciding what on earth to say to a person she had known for less time than it takes to boil an egg.

"Bacon sandwiches," she said eventually, mostly to herself.

Aala laughed, a proper, delighted laugh. "Hee hee hee. Got you."

It is, I should tell you, a very unusual way to begin a friendship. But then, the best ones often begin that way.

The path soon dipped through fern beds gone ragged at the edges, not the gentle raggedness of wind and weather, but the sorry, flattened raggedness of far too many hooves. Nova crouched to look. "It's not just a few animals," she said. "There's so many squished together that they are even tramping their food."

Aala's humor dropped away like a cloak shrugged off. "Then we'll be quick," they said, "and gentle where we can."

Deeper into the woods, things only got worse. Webwood silk hung where silk had no business hanging. Boar-scars tore up the soft earth. Saplings stood stripped and bent, like they'd been leaned on by something enormous, something thoroughly unbothered about the damage it was doing. Nova and Aala slowed at the edge of a grove gone thick with all this trouble, after a moment of saying nothing at all, the pushed forward.

The clearing beyond held the worst of it, four or five thistle boars packed into a space built for one, jostling like too many people trying to use the same doorway at once, with more shapes shifting in the brush beyond.

Aala's steadiness wavered, just for a heartbeat. "So many," they murmured. "They can't help the damage they are causing."

"No," Nova agreed. "But we can. Let's get this done."

She lifted her staff. She had practiced this spell a hundred times in empty rooms, on nothing more dangerous than dust motes, and never once on anything that could run away, or worse towards her with viscious tusks. A pale glow gathered at the staff's tip.

"A gentle push is all it should take," she said. "Watch."

And she let it go, not a blow, not a blast, nothing you'd write a song about, just a slow, curling pressure that eased around the boars like a polite but very firm usher clearing a theatre. The boars startled, shuffled, and peeled away toward the wider thickets, entirely unharmed and entirely unimpressed, which is really the best outcome you can hope for from a thistle boar.

Aala let out a long breath, shoulders loosening. "With that pack moved on," they said, watching the last one vanish, "hopefully the rest will find new homes too."

For a moment, the clearing was simply quiet, and quiet, as any storyteller will tell you, is when the important things tend to happen. Aala rested a hand near Nova's shoulder careful, testing, the way you'd offer a hand to someone you weren't yet sure would take it.

"You did amazingly, Nova, just the perfect amount of surprise to see them off."

Nova felt her face go warm. "I'm so glad we didn't hurt them," she admitted, more honestly than the moment probably called for. But Aala only nodded, as though that one small admission had told them something worth knowing.

They might have lingered there longer, if Aala hadn't crouched, a breath later, beside a moss-covered root and gone very still. Four deep gouges, freshly clawed into the wood.

"Take care," Nova said, all her ease folding back into alertness. "These are nightsaber hunting grounds. They can appear out of"

She never finished the sentence, because the shadows above them chose that exact moment to move.

"EEEEEEP, it's above us!" Aala's staff came up faster than thought, and a shimmering shielding ward flared into being overhead, just as a nightsaber's leap came down toward them, all claws and shadow and considerably more angry weight than either of them wanted to think about.

Nova didn't have time to be frightened. She only had time to cast a curling ribbon of pale light spooling out from her staff, bending gently around Aala's ward, catching the nightsaber's leap and steering it sideways, as harmlessly as a kitten bumping off your leg.

"Be gone," she said, and she said it kindly, if firmly, which is a very Nova sort of thing to do even to a beast trying to eat her.

The nightsaber twisted mid-air, landed hard but whole, shook itself off with the deeply offended air of a cat that has just missed a jump on purpose, and slunk away into the trees without so much as a backward glance.

For a moment, neither of them moved. Then Aala let their breath out in one long, shaky, delighted laugh, and lowered the ward.

"I think, that's the glen cleared." they said. "GG."

Now, Nova had no idea what those two letters meant. But she understood the feeling behind them perfectly well, that giddy, wobbly-kneed relief of having done something difficult and come out the other side of it whole, standing next to someone she had met only that morning and somehow already trusted with her life. She decided she didn't need to know what it meant. She suspected she'd be hearing it again.

They walked back to the moonwell slower than they'd left it, unhurried, the whole glen settling into a calm that hadn't been there before. Ilthalaine was waiting, and turned to greet them with the look of someone who already knew, somehow, exactly how the morning had gone, as if it was already determined before they had even woken up this morning.

"You've kept the balance," she said, drawing two folded cloaks from beneath her arm, moss-and-leaf toned, plain, and entirely new. "Wear these with pride."

Nova took hers with a quiet, surprised sort of pride she hadn't expected to feel over a piece of cloth. Aala accepted theirs with a grin, shrugging off the travel cloak they'd arrived in that morning as though it belonged to someone they'd already outgrown.

"Matching cloaks," Nova said, settling hers into place, fighting a smile of her own. "Try not to make it weird."

"No promises," said Aala.

And so they stood there together at the water's edge a little longer than strictly necessary, two strangers who had walked into Shadowglen's ferns that morning, and walked out of them as something else entirely. Not quite friends yet. But you could see the shape of it forming already, plain as two matching cloaks in the moonlight.
